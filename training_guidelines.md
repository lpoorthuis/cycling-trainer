## 1. Training Philosophy & Principles

### Guiding Principles

1. **Individuality first.** No two athletes adapt identically. Metabolic profiling (INSCYD, lactate testing, or field-based assessment) should inform every training decision. What works for Pogacar will not work for every GC rider, let alone a time-crunched amateur.

2. **Consistency over heroics.** Long-term aerobic development through Zone 2 is the foundation upon which all high-intensity performance is built. San Millan: *"Zone 2 training is the key training that unleashes the highest adaptations."*

3. **Specificity of stimulus.** Training must match the demands of the target event. A Grand Tour GC rider, a cobbled classics specialist, and a sprinter require fundamentally different physiological profiles.

4. **Progressive overload with recovery.** Stress the system, then let it adapt. Build in 3-4 week loading blocks followed by recovery weeks. Never increase weekly volume by more than 5-10%.

5. **Fuel the work required.** Nutrition is periodized alongside training: fully fuel high-intensity sessions, strategically restrict on select easy days. (Impey/Morton framework.)

6. **Off-bike work is non-negotiable.** Strength training improves cycling economy by ~5%, time to exhaustion by ~17%, and prevents overuse injuries. (Ronnestad meta-analysis, 2025.)

7. **Monitor, don't guess.** Track power, HR, HRV, RPE, wellness, and biomarkers. Adjust the plan based on the athlete's actual response, not the prescribed plan.

## 2. Physiological Profiling & Zone Models

### 2.1 The Two-Threshold Model (San Millan / Seiler)

All training is anchored by two physiological inflection points:

| Threshold | Marker | Typical Lactate | Significance |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| **LT1** (Aerobic Threshold) | Intensity where lactate first rises above baseline | ~1.7-2.0 mmol/L | Upper boundary of sustainable fat-burning zone |
| **LT2** (Anaerobic Threshold / MLSS) | Intensity where lactate accumulation outpaces clearance | ~4.0 mmol/L | Maximum sustainable steady-state intensity (~FTP) |

**Key insight:** Lactate is not a waste product -- it is an alternative fuel. Fast-twitch fibers export lactate via MCT4 transporters; slow-twitch mitochondria import it via MCT1 for oxidation. Training both thresholds improves this shuttle system.

### 2.2 Training Zone Framework

We use a hybrid 7-zone model aligned with power (% of FTP), while anchoring the critical decisions around LT1 and LT2:

| Zone | Name | % FTP | RPE (1-10) | Purpose |
|------|------|-------|------------|---------|
| Z1 | Active Recovery | <55% | 1-2 | Recovery, blood flow |
| Z2 | Endurance | 55-75% | 3-4 | Aerobic base, fat oxidation, mitochondrial density |
| Z3 | Tempo | 76-87% | 5-6 | Sustained aerobic work, muscular endurance |
| Z4 | Threshold | 88-105% | 7-8 | FTP development, lactate clearance at high power |
| Z5 | VO2max | 106-120% | 9 | Maximum aerobic capacity, cardiac output |
| Z6 | Anaerobic Capacity | 121-150% | 10 | Anaerobic power, lactate tolerance |
| Z7 | Neuromuscular Power | >150% | MAX | Sprint power, ATP-PC system, motor unit recruitment |

**Sweet Spot** (88-94% FTP) is the overlap between upper Z3 and lower Z4. Time-efficient but should not dominate a well-rounded program.

### 2.3 VLaMax and FatMax

**VLaMax (maximum glycolytic rate):** Determines how quickly an athlete burns through glycogen. Ranges from 0.2 mmol/L/s (marathon runners) to 1.0 mmol/L/s (100m sprinters).

- VO2max and VLaMax together determine approximately **97% of FTP**
- For road endurance cyclists, a **lower VLaMax** is generally desirable (improves threshold power, fat oxidation, glycogen sparing)
- Too low limits ability to produce explosive accelerations (important for sprinters, classics riders)

**FatMax:** The intensity at which fat oxidation peaks. Higher VO2max + lower VLaMax = higher FatMax power.

**Training to reduce VLaMax:** Long Zone 2 sessions, sweet spot / threshold intervals, reduced sprint work, specific training at FatMax intensity.

**Training to increase VLaMax (sprinters):** Short all-out sprints, anaerobic capacity intervals, plyometrics.

### 2.4 Metabolic Testing

**INSCYD** (used by Visma, Alpecin, BORA-hansgrohe, Movistar): Computes VO2max, VLaMax, MLSS, LT1, fat/carb combustion rates from a structured field test requiring only a power meter. Validation shows <2.5% error for VO2max vs. lab measurements.

**Lactate testing:** Gold standard for identifying LT1 and LT2. Performed with incremental ramp protocol and finger-prick blood samples.

**DFA Alpha1 (HRV-guided):** Crosses below 0.75 at aerobic threshold and below 0.50 at anaerobic threshold. Research shows ICC of 0.87-0.97, comparable to lab methods. Requires chest strap HR monitor (e.g., Polar H10).

**Recommended testing frequency:** At minimum every 12 weeks; elite athletes test 4-6 times per year to track metabolic shifts and adjust training accordingly.

## 3. Periodization & Season Structure

### 3.1 Evidence-Based Periodization

A 2025 network meta-analysis (co-authored by Seiler, *Sports Medicine*, 348 athletes) found **no difference** in VO2max or TT performance between polarized and pyramidal interventions -- but a periodized approach that transitions from pyramidal to polarized produced the greatest improvements (~0.5% better in TT performance). A landmark 2025 study of 28 WorldTour cyclists confirmed that intensity distribution is **pyramidal across all phases**, with progressive increases in high-intensity training toward competition.

**Recommended approach: Periodized Training Intensity Distribution (TID)**

| Phase | Distribution | Zone Focus |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| Off-season (Oct-Nov) | Very high Z1-Z2, minimal intensity | Aerobic base, strength |
| Base (Dec-Feb) | Pyramidal: 80-90% Z1-Z2, 10-15% Z3, 2-5% Z4-Z5 | Volume + sweet spot |
| Build (Mar-Apr) | Pyramidal shifting toward polarized: increasing Z4-Z5 | Threshold + VO2max |
| Competition (May-Sep) | Polarized: minimize Z3, emphasize Z1-Z2 + Z5-Z7 | Race-specific intensity |
| Transition (Oct) | Unstructured, low volume | Mental & physical rest |

### 3.2 Annual Season Framework

#### Phase 1: Transition / Off-Season (October -- 2-4 weeks)
- **Purpose:** Physical and mental recovery from racing season
- **Volume:** 40-60% of peak volume
- **Intensity:** None structured; ride for enjoyment
- **Off-bike:** Begin gym anatomical adaptation phase (3x15 reps, technique focus)
- **Cross-training welcome:** Running, swimming, hiking, cyclocross

#### Phase 2: General Preparation / Base (November-February -- 12-16 weeks)
- **Purpose:** Build aerobic foundation, develop strength, address limiters
- **Volume:** Gradually build from 60% to 90% of peak
- **Key sessions:**
  - 3-5 hour Zone 2 endurance rides (pro level: 5-6 hours)
  - Sweet spot intervals 2-3x/week (2x20, 3x15 at 88-94% FTP)
  - Introduction of threshold work in late base (2x20 at FTP)
- **Off-bike:** Heavy strength training 2x/week (progress to 4x4-6RM phase)
- **Intensity distribution:** ~85% Z1-Z2, ~12% Z3, ~3% Z4+

#### Phase 3: Specific Preparation / Build (February-April -- 8-12 weeks)
- **Purpose:** Develop race-specific fitness; raise VO2max and threshold
- **Volume:** 85-100% of peak volume
- **Key sessions:**
  - VO2max intervals (5x5, 4x8, or 30/15 format)
  - Threshold intervals (2x20 at 100-105% FTP)
  - Race-specific sessions (over-unders, race simulations)
  - Long rides with intensity blocks (tempo/threshold intervals in hours 3-5)
- **Off-bike:** Transition to power/explosive phase, then maintenance (1x/7-10 days)
- **Intensity distribution:** ~78% Z1-Z2, ~12% Z3, ~10% Z4-Z7

#### Phase 4: Competition (April-September)
- **Purpose:** Perform at peak; race as training stimulus
- **Volume:** Variable -- reduced during race blocks, maintained between
- **Key sessions:**
  - Openers before races (Z2 + 4x90s at FTP + 2x6s sprint)
  - Maintenance intervals between race blocks (2x per week)
  - Recovery rides post-race (Z1, 30-45 min)
- **Off-bike:** Gym maintenance 1x/7-10 days (skip race weeks)
- **Intensity distribution:** More polarized (races provide the intensity)

#### Phase 5: Targeted Peaking
- **3 weeks out:** Final hard training block (most specific, highest load)
- **2 weeks out:** Volume reduced 20-30%, intensity maintained
- **Race week:** Volume drops 40-60%, short sharp efforts only
- **Expected performance gain from proper taper:** 3-5%

## 4. Key On-Bike Workouts

### 4.1 Zone 2 / LT1 Endurance Rides

The foundation of all cycling training. Targets intensity just below LT1.

- **Intensity:** 55-75% FTP; lactate ~1.7-2.0 mmol/L; 60-70% max HR
- **Duration:** Minimum 90 min for meaningful stimulus; 3-5 hours for trained cyclists
- **Volume target:** 60-80% of weekly training time
- **Cadence:** Self-selected (typically 85-95 rpm)
- **Key adaptations:** Increased mitochondrial density, capillary growth, improved fat oxidation, enhanced lactate clearance, metabolic flexibility
- **Fueling:** Can be used as "train low" session (water only) for 60-90 min, or fueled (30-60 g/hr carbs) for longer rides
- **Pogacar's winter approach:** *"Zone 2, 5-6 hours, especially going alone and doing a big loop"*

### 4.2 Sweet Spot Intervals (88-94% FTP)

Time-efficient FTP builder. Sits just below threshold -- enough stimulus for adaptation, not so hard that recovery is compromised.

| Workout | Sets x Duration | Rest | Total Work |
|---------|----------------|------|------------|
| SS-20s | 2 x 20 min | 10 min | 40 min |
| SS-15s | 3 x 15 min | 8 min | 45 min |
| SS-10s | 4 x 10 min | 5 min | 40 min |

- **When to use:** Base building with limited time; gravel/century/stage race prep
- **Frequency:** 2-3x/week maximum
- **Limitation:** Does not develop VO2max or top-end power; can become monotonous and lead to plateau if overused

### 4.3 Threshold Intervals (95-105% FTP)

Develops the ability to sustain high power at lactate threshold.

| Workout | Sets x Duration | Rest | Total Work |
|---------|----------------|------|------------|
| Classic 2x20 | 2 x 20 min | 10 min | 40 min |
| Build 3x15 | 3 x 15 min | 8 min | 45 min |
| Stepper 4x10 | 4 x 10 min | 5 min | 40 min |
| Boardman Ladder | 15+12+10+8 min | 5 min | 45 min |

- **Progression:** Start with 3x10, advance to 4x10, then 3x15, then 2x20
- **Weekly threshold volume target:** 40-60 min per session, 90-120 min per week
- **When to use:** Build and race phases; critical for TT, crit, and track

### 4.4 VO2max Intervals (106-120% FTP)

The most potent stimulus for raising the aerobic ceiling.

#### Long Intervals
| Workout | Sets x Duration | Intensity | Rest | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-----------|------|-------|
| Seiler 4x8 | 4 x 8 min | 106% FTP | 4 min | Research shows superior VO2max gains |
| Classic 5x5 | 5 x 5 min | 108% FTP | 5 min | Widely used gold standard |
| Extended 3x8 | 3 x 8 min | 106% FTP | 6 min | Good for less-trained athletes |

#### Short Intervals
| Workout | Format | Intensity | Rest Intensity | Total Duration |
|---------|--------|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| Billat 30/30 | 30s ON / 30s OFF | 130-140% FTP | 55-60% FTP | 16-36 min |
| Ronnestad 30/15 | 30s ON / 15s OFF | 130-140% FTP | 55-60% FTP | 16-24 min |
| Classic 40/20 | 40s ON / 20s OFF | 120-130% FTP | 55-60% FTP | 16-24 min |

**Ronnestad 30/15 research:** Improved FTP by 5% more than traditional 5x5 intervals in 3 weeks. Cutting rest to 15 seconds provides more time above 90% VO2max.

**Hard-start technique:** Begin each interval with a 30-second burst at 130% FTP, then settle to target intensity. This drives VO2 to near-max faster, increasing effective time at VO2max.

### 4.5 Over-Under Intervals

Develops lactate clearance and buffering capacity. Simulates race surges.

| Workout | Structure | "Over" | "Under" | Total |
|---------|-----------|--------|---------|-------|
| Bear Creek | 3 x 12 min: 2 min over / 2 min under | 105-110% FTP | 90-95% FTP | 36 min |
| Crit Sim | 3 x 12 min: 1 min over / 1 min under | 110% FTP | 90% FTP | 36 min |
| GC Climb Sim | 2 x 20 min: 1 min over / 4 min under | 115% FTP | 100% FTP | 40 min |

### 4.6 Anaerobic Capacity (121-150% FTP, 30s-3 min)

Critical for covering attacks, bridging gaps, short steep climbs.

| Workout | Sets x Duration | Intensity | Rest |
|---------|----------------|-----------|------|
| Short Repeats | 8-12 x 30s | Max sustainable | 4-5 min |
| Attack Sim | 6 x 1 min | 140-150% FTP | 5 min |
| Bridge Gaps | 5 x 2 min | 130% FTP | 5 min |
| Classics Surge | 3 sets of 6 x 1 min ON / 1 min OFF | Max sustainable | 5 min between sets |

### 4.7 Sprint / Neuromuscular Power (>150% FTP, <15s)

- **Form sprints:** Mid-range gear, wind cadence from normal to max in <10s, seated and standing. 6-8 reps, full recovery
- **Standing-start stomps:** Hardest gear, turn over without exceeding 90 rpm in 12 pedal strokes. 5x 10-12s, full recovery
- **Downhill sprints:** Roll in at 37+ km/h in a big gear (100+ rpm), jump out of saddle, sprint 20s. 5x with 5-min recovery
- **Repeated sprint blocks:** 6x 10s sprints with 10s recovery (targets sprint fatigue resistance)

### 4.8 Race Simulation & Fatigue Resistance

- **GC race sim:** 4-5 hour ride with 4x15 min at FTP on climbs in hours 3-5
- **Domestique endurance:** 3x60 min at tempo within a long ride
- **UAE hybrid session:** 2x20 min blocks: 1 min at 115% FTP + 4 min at 100% FTP, repeating
- **Visma hybrid session:** 3x25 min sets: 15s sprint + 6 min tempo, repeating
- **Fatigue resistance test:** Compare 5-min power after 1,000 kJ vs. 5,000 kJ of accumulated work

## 5. Strength & Gym Training

### 5.1 Why Strength Training Is Essential

The evidence is now unequivocal. A 2025 meta-analysis (European Journal of Applied Physiology, 262 participants) confirmed that heavy strength training (>=80% 1RM) improves physiological determinants of endurance cycling performance:

- **Cycling economy:** +4.8% (less oxygen cost per watt)
- **Time to exhaustion at MAP:** +17.2%
- **5-min all-out power after prolonged cycling:** Significant improvement
- **No change in VO2max or body weight** (strength gains are neuromuscular, not hypertrophic at these loads)

**Key distinction:** High-rep strength work is essentially redundant for cyclists -- the gains come from heavy loads and low reps that improve motor unit recruitment and neuromuscular efficiency.

### 5.2 Key Exercises

#### Primary Lower Body (Bilateral)
| Exercise | Primary Muscles | Notes |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| Back Squat / Half Squat | Quads, glutes, hamstrings | Most-studied exercise in cycling research. Ronnestad's protocols used half-squats. |
| Front Squat | Quads, glutes, core | More upright torso, less lower back stress |
| Trap Bar Deadlift | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, erectors | Spine-friendlier than conventional deadlift |
| Leg Press | Quads, glutes | Used in Ronnestad's research; single-leg version addresses imbalances |
| Hip Thrust | Gluteus maximus | Band around knees encourages stability; strengthens glutes while stretching hip flexors |

#### Primary Lower Body (Unilateral)
| Exercise | Primary Muscles | Notes |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| Bulgarian Split Squat | Quads, glutes, hip stabilizers | 70-85% force through front leg. Addresses bilateral imbalances. Start with bodyweight. |
| Romanian Deadlift (RDL) | Hamstrings, glutes | Single-leg RDL realigns femoral head and strengthens glutes |
| Weighted Step-Ups | Quads, glutes | Functional single-leg exercise mimicking pedaling mechanics |

#### Upper Body
| Exercise | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Push-ups | Anterior core, chest, triceps; supports aero position |
| Pull-ups / Lat Pulldown | Posture, handlebar pulling during climbing/sprinting |
| Bent-Over / Dumbbell Rows | Posterior shoulder, upper back; prevents neck problems. At least 2x/week. |
| Overhead Press | Shoulder stability, posture support |

### 5.3 Periodized Gym Program

#### Phase 1: Anatomical Adaptation (2-4 weeks, October)
- **Purpose:** Prepare body for lifting, perfect technique
- **Load:** 3 x 15 reps at RPE 5-6
- **Frequency:** 2-3x/week

**Session A (Monday):**
1. Back Squat: 3x15
2. Romanian Deadlift: 3x12
3. Leg Press (single leg): 3x12/side
4. Push-ups: 3x12-15
5. Bent-over Rows: 3x12
6. Core circuit: 10 min

**Session B (Thursday):**
1. Front Squat: 3x15
2. Bulgarian Split Squat: 3x12/side (bodyweight)
3. Hip Thrust: 3x15
4. Pull-ups or Lat Pulldown: 3x8-12
5. Overhead Press: 3x12
6. Core circuit: 10 min

#### Phase 2: Hypertrophy / Strength Build (6-8 weeks, Nov-Dec)
- **Purpose:** Build functional strength base
- **Load:** Progress from 3x10 @ 65% 1RM to 4x6 @ 80% 1RM
- **Rest:** 2-3 minutes between sets
- **Frequency:** 2x/week

**Session A:**
1. Back Squat: Build from 3x10 @ 65% to 4x6 @ 80%
2. Romanian Deadlift: 3x8 @ 70-75%
3. Single-leg Leg Press: 3x10/side
4. Hip Thrust: 3x10 @ 70%
5. Push-ups: 3x15-20
6. Dumbbell Rows: 3x10/side

**Session B:**
1. Front Squat: Build from 3x10 @ 60% to 4x6 @ 75%
2. Bulgarian Split Squat: Progress to 3x8/side with dumbbells
3. Step-ups (weighted): 3x10/side
4. Single-leg Glute Bridge: 3x10/side
5. Pull-ups: 3x max reps
6. Core circuit: 10 min

#### Phase 3: Maximum Strength (6-8 weeks, Dec-Jan)
- **Purpose:** Peak neuromuscular strength; improve motor unit recruitment
- **Load:** 4 x 4-6 reps @ 80-90% 1RM
- **Rest:** 3-5 minutes between sets (full neural recovery)
- **Frequency:** 2x/week

**Session A:**
1. Back Squat or Half Squat: 4x4-6 @ 80-90% (Ronnestad protocol)
2. Trap Bar Deadlift: 3x5 @ 80-85%
3. Single-leg Leg Press: 3x6/side @ 80%
4. Bent-over Rows: 3x6 @ 80%
5. Core: Pallof press 3x10/side, Dead bug 3x8/side

**Session B:**
1. Front Squat: 4x4-6 @ 80-85%
2. Bulgarian Split Squat: 3x6/side with barbell
3. Hip Thrust: 3x6 @ 80-85%
4. Weighted Pull-ups: 3x5
5. Core: Side planks 3x30s/side, Bird dogs 3x8/side

#### Phase 4: Power / Explosive (4-6 weeks, Feb)
- **Purpose:** Convert strength to sport-specific power
- **Load:** 3-4 x 3-5 reps, explosive concentric intent
- **Rest:** 3-5 minutes
- **Frequency:** 2x/week transitioning to 1x/week

**Session A:**
1. Back Squat: 3x4-5 @ 80-85%, explosive concentric
2. Squat Jumps: 3x5 (bodyweight or light load)
3. Romanian Deadlift: 3x6 @ 75-80%
4. Box Jumps: 3x5 (focus on soft, quiet landing)
5. Core circuit: 8 min

**Session B (lighter):**
1. Bulgarian Split Squat: 3x6/side @ 70%
2. Hip Thrust: 3x8 @ 70%
3. Step-ups: 3x8/side
4. Pull-ups: 3x6-8
5. Push-ups: 3x15
6. Core: 8 min

#### Phase 5: In-Season Maintenance (Mar-Oct)
- **Purpose:** Preserve strength gains during racing
- **Frequency:** 1 session every 7-10 days (skip race weeks)
- **Critical finding:** Gains erode within 8 weeks of complete cessation (Ronnestad, 2015-2016)

**Maintenance Session:**
1. Back Squat: 3x3-5 @ RPE 7-8
2. Romanian Deadlift: 2x5
3. Bulgarian Split Squat: 2x6/side
4. Core: Dead bugs 2x8/side, Pallof press 2x8/side

**Scheduling rules:**
- Place gym session on a non-race, non-hard-ride day
- Follow with rest or easy day
- Allow at least 5 hours between riding and strength training (interference effect)
- Final strength session before a key race: 2-4 days out, volume halved, intensity maintained

### 5.4 Rider-Type Strength Priorities

| Factor | Sprinter | Climber | GC Rider |
|--------|----------|---------|----------|
| Gym focus | Explosive power, lean mass | Injury prevention, joint stability | Capillary development, robustness |
| Gym volume | Higher (up to 3-4x/week for track) | Lower (1-2x/week) | Moderate (2x/week) |
| Exercise style | Explosive, move weight fast | Imbalance correction, joint protection | Balanced strength + endurance |
| Plyometrics | High priority | Lower priority (bone density benefit) | Moderate priority |

### 5.5 Plyometrics & Explosive Work

**Prerequisites:** Complete at least 6-8 weeks of resistance training before starting plyometrics.

**Research evidence:** A study on well-trained cyclists showed after 12 plyometric sessions: +8.1% in 1km power, +6.8% in peak power, +3.7% in lactate threshold power, -3% in total oxygen cost.

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Notes |
|----------|------------|-------|
| Squat Jumps | 3-4 x 5 | Full hip/knee extension at top; land quietly |
| Box Jumps | 3-4 x 5 | Soft landings; progress height gradually |
| Depth Jumps (advanced) | 3 x 5 | Only after 6-8 weeks of base plyometrics |
| Single-Leg Hops | 3 x 8/side | Sport-specific single-leg power transfer |

**When:** Late off-season / early pre-season. Never on excessively fatigued muscles. 2-3 sessions/week maximum during a dedicated block.

## 6. Core Training

### 6.1 Principles

Core stability is essential for **power transfer** from trunk to legs and for maintaining an efficient position. Without adequate core endurance, the pelvis rocks, the lower back fatigues, and power leaks through the kinetic chain.

Focus on **anti-movement** patterns (anti-extension, anti-rotation, anti-lateral flexion) rather than crunches or sit-ups.

### 6.2 Key Exercises

| Category | Exercise | Sets x Reps/Duration |
|----------|----------|---------------------|
| **Anti-Extension** | Front Plank | 3-5 x 30-60s (progress to 2 min) |
| | Dead Bugs | 3 x 8-12/side |
| | Ab Rollouts | 3 x 8-10 |
| **Anti-Rotation** | Pallof Press | 3 x 10-12/side |
| | Pallof Dead Bug (combined) | 3 x 8/side |
| **Anti-Lateral Flexion** | Side Plank | 3 x 30-45s/side |
| | Copenhagen Plank | 2 x 20-30s/side |
| **Dynamic** | Bird Dogs | 3 x 8-12/side |
| | Windshield Wipers | 3 x 8/side |
| **Posterior Chain** | Glute Bridge / Single-Leg Bridge | 3 x 12-15 |

### 6.3 Sample Core Circuit (10 minutes)

Perform as a circuit or finisher after strength training, or as a standalone session:

1. Dead bugs: 3 x 10/side
2. Side plank: 2 x 30s/side
3. Pallof press: 2 x 10/side
4. Bird dogs: 2 x 10/side
5. Front plank: 2 x 45s

**Frequency:** 2x/week optimal (can embed in gym sessions); reduce to 1x/week in-season.

## 7. Activation & Warm-Up Protocols

### 7.1 Pre-Ride Activation (5 minutes)

Cyclists spend hours in hip flexion, which inhibits glutes. This protocol "wakes up" the primary engines before riding.

1. **Glute Bridges:** 10 reps, 2s hold at top. Squeeze glutes, don't push through lower back.
2. **Clamshells:** 10 reps/side. Lying on side, knees bent, open and close top leg.
3. **Banded Monster Walks:** 20 steps (10 each direction). Mini band above knees, slight knee bend.
4. **Fire Hydrants:** 8 reps/side from quadruped position.
5. **Glute Bridge March:** 8 reps/side. Hold bridge position, alternately lift each foot.

**Key principle:** Wake up muscles WITHOUT pre-fatiguing them. Slow, controlled, deliberate reps.

### 7.2 Dynamic Warm-Up (Pre-Intensity Sessions)

Before hard interval sessions or races, add dynamic movements:

1. Leg swings (front-to-back): 10/side
2. Leg swings (side-to-side): 10/side
3. Walking lunges: 10 total
4. High knees: 20 steps
5. Torso rotations: 10/side
6. Arm circles: 10 forward, 10 backward

### 7.3 Pre-Race / TT Warm-Up Protocol (25-45 minutes on trainer)

**General rule:** Shorter race = longer warm-up.

1. 5-8 min easy spinning (Zone 1, RPE 2-3)
2. 8 min Zone 2 (progressive build)
3. 5-6 min Zone 3 (tempo)
4. 4 min at/near threshold
5. 3 min easy recovery
6. 2-3 x 10-30s sub-max sprints with 1 min recovery
7. 5 min easy Zone 2/3 spinning
8. Off trainer 5-10 min before start

**Combined protocol:** 5-min off-bike activation (glute bridges, clamshells, dynamic stretches) before mounting trainer.

## 8. Mobility & Flexibility

### 8.1 Priority Areas for Cyclists

Cycling locks the body into a single plane for hours, creating predictable tightness patterns:

| Area | Why It's Tight | Impact |
|------|---------------|--------|
| Hip flexors (iliopsoas) | Sustained hip flexion on bike | Inhibits glutes, causes anterior pelvic tilt, lower back pain |
| Thoracic spine | Rounded forward position | Restricts breathing, shoulder/neck pain |
| Quadriceps | Thousands of repetitions without full extension | Patellar compression, reduced ROM |
| Hamstrings | Shortened range, repetitive use | Lower back pain, pedaling inefficiency |
| IT band / TFL | Repetitive knee flexion/extension | Lateral knee pain |
| Calves | Repeated plantarflexion under load | Achilles issues, reduced ankle mobility |
| Pectorals | Sustained forward reach | Rounded shoulders, neck pain |

### 8.2 Foam Rolling Protocol (8-12 minutes post-ride)

Roll each area for 60-120 seconds. Slow passes at ~1 inch per second. If you find a painful spot, stop and rest on it for 10 seconds, then continue.

**Target areas (in order):**
1. Quads
2. Hip flexors / TFL
3. Glutes / piriformis
4. Hamstrings
5. Calves
6. Upper back (thoracic spine)

**NEVER foam roll the lower back directly.** The lumbar spine lacks bony protection. Instead, roll the hip flexors, glutes, and piriformis, which often refer tension to the lower back.

**Timing:** Post-ride rolling has more evidence for reducing soreness. Pre-ride rolling can be part of warm-up but keep it brief (30-45s per area).

### 8.3 Static Stretching (Post-Ride or Rest Days)

Hold each position 30-60 seconds. Do NOT static stretch before high-intensity efforts (temporarily reduces force production).

| Stretch | Target | Duration |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Kneeling hip flexor stretch | Iliopsoas | 60s/side |
| Pigeon pose | Hip flexors, rotators | 60-120s/side |
| Quad stretch (lying or standing) | Quadriceps | 30-60s/side |
| Hamstring stretch (doorway or supine) | Hamstrings | 30-60s/side |
| Wall calf stretch (straight leg) | Gastrocnemius | 30s/side |
| Wall calf stretch (bent knee) | Soleus | 30s/side |
| Pec stretch (doorway) | Pectorals | 30-60s/side |
| Upper trap / levator scapulae stretch | Neck/shoulders | 30s/side |

### 8.4 Key Mobility Drills

**Hip Mobility:**
- **90/90 Stretch:** Sit with front leg at 90 degrees external rotation, back leg at 90 degrees internal rotation. Hinge forward over front knee. 3-5 sets/side.
- **Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs):** Stand on one leg, slowly draw full circles with the other knee. 3-5 reps each direction, daily.
- **Pigeon Pose:** Deep hip opener. 60-120 seconds/side.

**Thoracic Spine:**
- **Foam Roller Extensions:** Lie back with roller at mid-back, support head, gently extend. 10-15 reps.
- **Thread the Needle:** From hands-and-knees, slide one arm under the body. 8-10 reps/side.
- **Cat-Cow:** Dynamic spinal mobility. 10 reps, daily.

**Ankle:**
- **Eccentric Heel Drops:** Lift both heels, lower slowly. 3 x 8-12 reps.
- **Ankle CARs:** Full circles exploring range of motion. 3-5 reps each direction.

### 8.5 Yoga Integration

| Timing | Style | Focus |
|--------|-------|-------|
| After rides | Yin / restorative | 20-30 min: pigeon, supine twist, legs up the wall |
| Between rides | Flow / vinyasa | Heat-building, mobility through movement |
| Rest days | Yin yoga | Long holds (2-5 min) reaching connective tissue |
| Before bed (hard training days) | Restorative | 15 min: legs up the wall + supine twist |

## 9. Injury Prevention

### 9.1 Common Overuse Injuries & Prevention

#### Patellar Tendinopathy (15-33% of endurance cyclists)
- **Causes:** Saddle too low, over-gearing, sudden volume increases
- **Prevention:**
  - Saddle height: 25-35 degrees knee flexion at bottom dead center
  - Eccentric squats on decline board: 3x15, slow 3-4 second lowering
  - Terminal knee extensions with band
  - Increase weekly volume by max 10%

#### IT Band Syndrome
- **Causes:** Hip abductor weakness, tight TFL, improper Q-factor/cleat setup
- **Prevention:**
  - Clamshells with band: 3x15/side
  - Side-lying hip abduction: 3x12/side
  - Single-leg bridges: 3x10/side
  - Foam roll TFL (not directly over IT band)

#### Lower Back Pain (53% of pro road cyclists report in a given year)
- **Causes:** Sustained forward flexion, weak core, tight hip flexors
- **Prevention:**
  - Glute bridges: 3x15
  - Dead bugs: 3x10/side
  - Single-leg RDL: 3x8/side
  - Planks: 3x45-60s
  - Hip flexor stretching post-ride

#### Neck / Shoulder Pain (23% of cyclists)
- **Causes:** Sustained forward head posture, excessive reach/drop
- **Prevention:**
  - Chin tucks (neck retraction): 3x10, hold 5s
  - Dumbbell rows: 3x10/side, 2x/week
  - Angel wings (wall angels): 3x10
  - Thoracic spine extensions over foam roller

### 9.2 Bike Fit

Correct bike fit is the **first line of defense**. Key parameters:

| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Saddle height | 145-150 degrees knee angle at BDC | Adjust in 2-3mm increments, 1+ week between changes |
| Cleat position | Pedal spindle under ball of foot | When in doubt, move cleats rearward |
| Q-factor | Hip-knee-foot in a straight line | Road typical: 150mm; MTB: 170mm |
| Reach/Stack | Comfortable, sustainable position | Excessive reach/drop = neck/shoulder pain, lower back stress |

**Fit is not static.** Training volume, flexibility, injury, and equipment changes all influence ideal positioning. Even small errors multiply over thousands of pedal strokes.

## 10. Nutrition & Fueling

### 10.1 Daily Carbohydrate Periodization ("Fuel for the Work Required")

The foundational framework from Impey, Close, Morton (Liverpool John Moores / Team Sky): Carbohydrate availability should be manipulated day-to-day according to session intensity and goals.

| Session Type | Daily Carb Target (g/kg) | In-Training Intake (g/hr) |
|--------------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Rest / very easy day | 3-4 | N/A |
| Low-intensity endurance | 5-6 | 0-30 (can be "train low") |
| Moderate training day | 6-8 | 30-60 |
| Hard interval / race-sim day | 8-10 | 60-90 |
| Carb-loading pre-race | 10-12 (for 36-48 hrs) | Practice race protocol |

**"Sleep Low, Train Low" model:** Evening training, restrict carbohydrate overnight, complete a fasted low-intensity session the following morning. Total time with reduced glycogen: 12-14 hours. Use sparingly (30-50% of easy sessions maximum).

### 10.2 Fasted Rides

**When appropriate:**
- Low intensity only (Zone 2), max 60-90 minutes
- Maximum 1-2 times per week
- Best during base/off-season phase

**When harmful:**
- Any high-intensity session
- Too frequently (impairs carbohydrate oxidation capacity)
- Riders with history of disordered eating
- Sessions exceeding 90 minutes without fueling

### 10.3 High Carbohydrate Intake & Gut Training

Modern elite cycling has shifted dramatically toward very high in-race carbohydrate intake. UAE Team Emirates: *"10 years ago, everyone was consuming 60g/hr. Now we're doing 110-120g/hr as standard."*

**The science of dual transport:**
- Glucose absorbed via SGLT1 (max ~60 g/hr)
- Fructose uses separate GLUT5 transporters
- Combined = maximum oxidation of ~1.5 g/min (90 g/hr+)
- **Current optimal ratio:** 1:0.8 glucose:fructose supports 100-120 g/hr

**Gut Training Protocol (8+ weeks):**

| Weeks | Target | Focus |
|-------|--------|-------|
| 1-2 | 40-60 g/hr | Baseline with familiar foods |
| 3-4 | ~90 g/hr | 2:1 glucose:fructose ratio |
| 5-6 | 90-100 g/hr | Shift toward 1:0.8 ratio |
| 7-8 | 100-120 g/hr | Practice under race intensity |
| Ongoing | Race protocol | Simulate weekly |

### 10.4 Race Nutrition

**Pre-race carb loading (48-72 hours):**
- 8-10 g/kg/day of low-fiber carbohydrates
- For 70 kg rider: 490-700g carbs/day

**Race morning (3-4 hours before):**
- 100-150g easy-to-digest carbohydrate
- Common pro choices: steamed rice, eggs, avocado, pancakes
- Espresso for caffeine

**During-race fueling:**

| Race Type | Duration | Carb Target (g/hr) |
|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| Time Trial | 30-60 min | Minimal (pre-loaded) |
| Criterium | 45-90 min | 30-60 |
| Road Race | 3-5 hrs | 60-90 (aim for 90+) |
| Grand Tour flat stage | 4-5 hrs | 60-90 |
| Grand Tour mountain stage | 4-6 hrs | 90-120 |

**Post-race recovery (start within 30 minutes):**

| Nutrient | Target | Timing |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Carbohydrate | 1.0-1.2 g/kg/hr for 4-6 hours | Within 30 min |
| Protein | 0.3-0.4 g/kg (20-40g) | Within 30-60 min |
| Fluid | 125-150% of fluid lost | Immediately |
| Sodium | 1g per kg body weight lost | With rehydration |

### 10.5 Body Composition

**W/kg Benchmarks:**

| Level | FTP (W/kg) |
|-------|-----------|
| Recreational cyclist | 2.5-3.0 |
| Competitive amateur | 3.5-4.5 |
| Cat 1/2 racer | 4.5+ |
| WorldTour pro | 5.5-6.5 |
| GC contender | ~6.0-6.9 (15-min efforts) |

**Safe weight loss principles:**
- Maximum ~0.5 kg/week via 300-500 kcal daily deficit
- Best timing: off-season / base phase (never during competition)
- Maintain protein at 1.6-2.1 g/kg/day to preserve muscle
- No more than 8% weight gain over competition weight during off-season
- Focus on body composition, not scale weight

**RED-S awareness (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport):**
RED-S causes reduced metabolic rate, compromised bone density, impaired immunity, loss of cognitive function, and increased injury risk. Early weight loss can mask damage -- performance may initially improve before form collapses. A stronger rider 2-5 kg heavier is always better than a weaker rider who hasn't improved power.

### 10.6 Hydration

- **Pre-ride:** 5-7 mL/kg fluid with 20 mg sodium/kg, ~90 min before
- **During:** Sip 150 mL every 10 minutes; 500-700 mg sodium/hr
- **Acceptable loss:** Stay below 2% body mass
- **Post-exercise:** Replace 125-150% of fluid lost + 1g sodium per kg lost
- **Sweat rate:** Test in multiple conditions (average ~1.28 L/hr warm conditions)
- **Sweat sodium:** 200-2,000 mg/L (largely genetic; professional testing recommended)

### 10.7 Supplements (Evidence-Based)

#### Proven Ergogenic Aids

| Supplement | Dose | Timing | Effect |
|-----------|------|--------|--------|
| **Caffeine** | 3-6 mg/kg | 45-60 min pre-exercise | Improved power, endurance, reduced RPE |
| **Sodium Bicarbonate** | 0.3 g/kg acute OR 0.3-0.5 g/kg/day serial (3-5 days) | 1-3 hrs pre-competition | Buffers blood acidity; best for 1-10 min efforts |
| **Beetroot Juice** | 310-560 mg nitrate (1-2 x 70 mL shots) | 2-3 hrs pre-exercise; 3-7 day loading for elite | ~3% endurance improvement (less in highly trained) |
| **Beta-Alanine** | 3.2-6.4 g/day loading (4-10 weeks), 1.2 g/day maintenance | With meals | Small benefit (0.2-3%) for 30s-10 min efforts |
| **Creatine** | 2-5 g/day (skip loading to minimize water weight) | Off-season strength phase | Sprint power; rarely used by road pros due to weight |

#### Daily Health Supplements (What WorldTour teams actually use)

| Supplement | Dose | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| Vitamin D | 2,000-4,000 IU (winter); 1,000-2,000 IU (summer) | Target 75-100 nmol/L via blood tests |
| Omega-3 | Daily | Anti-inflammatory, recovery |
| Magnesium | Daily | Cofactor for 300+ enzymes |
| Zinc | Daily | Immune support |
| Iron | Only if deficient (blood-test guided) | Target ferritin >= 100 ng/mL; take with vitamin C, away from caffeine |

#### Pre-Sleep Recovery Protocol

- 40-48g casein protein 30 min before bed (increases overnight muscle protein synthesis)
- Tart cherry juice (300 mg polyphenols): natural melatonin + anti-inflammatory anthocyanins
- Use during peak training/racing, not daily (chronic high-dose antioxidants may blunt training adaptations)

## 11. Recovery Strategies

### 11.1 Sleep (The #1 Recovery Tool)

- **Minimum:** 8 hours/night; illness risk nearly doubles below this
- **Naps:** 20-60 min mid-afternoon after long rides (HGH secreted during slow-wave sleep)
- **Environment:** Dark, cool room; consistent bedtime; limit afternoon caffeine
- **Pro examples:** EF Pro Cycling provides Eight Sleep temperature-controlled mattresses at home and Grand Tours; Tudor Pro Cycling provides recovery pillows
- **Breathing technique:** 4-second nose inhale / 8-second mouth exhale (British Cycling sleep coach)

### 11.2 Active Recovery Rides

- Zone 1 for 30-45 minutes
- Brings oxygenated blood to muscles, reduces stiffness
- Minimum one full rest or recovery day per week

### 11.3 Cold Water Immersion

- **Protocol:** 10-15 minutes at 5-10 degrees C
- **Benefits:** Reduces DOMS and muscle damage markers (CK)
- **Compatible with nutrition:** Does not impair glycogen resynthesis
- **Use strategically:** Most beneficial during competition blocks and between hard back-to-back sessions

### 11.4 Compression & Massage

- **Pressotherapy boots:** Used on team buses post-stage for blood flow and swelling reduction
- **Massage:** Improves pain sensation; important for preventing low back/neck problems
- **Foam rolling post-ride:** 8-12 minutes on quads, hip flexors, glutes, hamstrings, calves, thoracic spine

### 11.5 Recovery Periodization

- Extended rest/recovery every 3-4 weeks within training blocks
- Many WorldTour teams use 4-5 week build blocks followed by deload weeks (not strict 3:1)
- During deload week: reduce volume 40-50%, maintain some intensity, prioritize sleep and nutrition

## 12. Goal-Specific Training

### 12.1 Grand Tour GC

**Target profile:** ~6.0-6.5 W/kg FTP, VO2max 70-90 ml/kg/min, low VLaMax, exceptional metabolic efficiency

**Season structure:**
- Nov-Feb: Aerobic base + heavy strength training + altitude camp(s)
- Mar-Apr: Build phase with B-races (Tirreni-Adriatico, Volta ao Algarve, Paris-Nice)
- May: Final altitude camp (Tenerife / Sierra Nevada), 3-4 weeks
- Jun: Prep race (Criterium du Dauphine / Tour de Suisse)
- Jul-Sep: Grand Tour target(s)

**Key sessions:**
- Long Zone 2 rides: 5-6 hours (foundation)
- Threshold intervals: 2x20 at 100% FTP (on climbs)
- VO2max development: 5x5 at 108% FTP; 4x8 at 106% FTP
- GC race simulation: 4x15 min at FTP on climbs in hours 3-5 of a long ride
- Over-under climb simulation: 2x20 min blocks (1 min at 115% FTP + 4 min at 100% FTP)
- Back-to-back weekend rides for multi-day adaptation

**Altitude camps (2,000-2,500m):**
- Minimum 14 days; optimal 3-4 weeks
- Expected: +8% hemoglobin mass, +4% VO2max
- Performance window: weeks 1-4 post-descent
- Ensure adequate iron stores before camp

**Double periodization (two Grand Tours):**
- Athletes can peak 2-3 times per year
- 6-8 week build-up to each peak
- Racing between targets serves as high-intensity training
- Example: Pogacar Giro+Tour 2024; Vingegaard planning Giro+Tour 2026

### 12.2 Time Trialing

**Target profile:** High sustained power at 90-100% FTP for race duration; exceptional W/CdA ratio

**Key principle:** TT performance = W/CdA (watts relative to aerodynamic drag). Evenepoel's raw power is less than Ganna's yet he can be faster due to superior aerodynamics.

**Key sessions:**
- Sustained threshold: 3x15 or 4x10 min at 95-100% FTP (in aero position)
- Sweet spot endurance: 2x30 min at 88-94% FTP
- Race-pace practice TTs: 48-56 min at 95-102% FTP
- Low-cadence strength: 4-6 x 4 min at 40-60 rpm in Z3-Z4
- VO2max for TT: 6x3 min at 110-120% FTP

**Position-specific:** Spend significant training time in aero position. At least 1-2 outdoor rides/week on TT bike. Indoor trainer provides controlled environment for position work.

**Pacing:** Even or slight negative split. Start at 90-94% FTP, settle in for 60 seconds. Evenepoel hit within 1 second of his pre-race target at 2024 Worlds.

### 12.3 One-Day Classics (Cobbles & Ardennes)

**Target profile:** High anaerobic capacity alongside strong aerobic base; explosive power for short climbs; ability to recover between repeated surges

**How Van der Poel prepares:**
- Uses cyclocross (12-13 races) as winter racing base (maintains intensity, bike handling)
- Transition to road: endurance + gym work post-CX Worlds
- Road prep: Tirreni-Adriatico / Paris-Nice
- Classics block: E3, Gent-Wevelgem, Tour of Flanders, Paris-Roubaix

**Key sessions:**
- Explosive power: 6x 30-40s at max sustainable power, 5-7 min recovery
- Repeated efforts: 3 sets of 6x 1 min ON / 1 min OFF, 5 min between sets
- Sprint-specific: 6x 10s maximal with 10s recovery
- Combined aerobic-anaerobic: 30s anaerobic repeats alternated with 7 min at 95% FTP
- Classics surge simulation: ride at 115% FTP for 1 min, 100% FTP for 3 min, repeat

**Reconnaissance:** Essential for Belgian events. Teams ride cobbled sectors to identify optimal lines, tactical positions, equipment decisions. Route knowledge can determine winning vs. losing.

### 12.4 Climbing

**Target profile:** High W/kg (body weight minimized while maintaining power), high fractional utilization of VO2max, excellent economy

**Key sessions:**
- Long sustained threshold: 3x15 min or 2x30 min at 100% FTP on real climbs
- Climb simulation: 1x 40-60 min at 85-90% FTP on sustained grade
- Above-threshold surges: 4x8 min at 106% FTP (the race-winning move on a climb lasts 3-6 min)
- Power Threes: 5x 3 min maximal on 4-10% grade hills
- Low-cadence high-torque: trains fast-twitch fibers to assist aerobically

**Weight management:** 1 pound of excess body weight costs ~2 watts on a climb. 10 lbs loss = 7-10% faster climbing. But focus on body composition, not just scale weight. Never sacrifice power for weight.

### 12.5 Sprinting

**Target profile:** High peak power (1,500-1,900W for WorldTour sprinters), rapid rate of force development, neuromuscular efficiency

**Key sessions:**
- Form sprints: mid-range gear, wind cadence to max in <10s. 6-8 reps, full recovery
- Standing-start stomps: hardest gear, <90 rpm for 12 pedal strokes. 5x 10-12s
- Downhill sprints: roll in at 37+ km/h, jump and sprint 20s. 5x with 5 min recovery
- Repeated sprint blocks: 6x 10s with 10s recovery
- High-cadence drills: target 200+ rpm (neuromuscular coordination)

**Gym work for sprinters:**
- Higher priority than other rider types
- Explosive squats, plyometrics, squat jumps, box jumps
- Upon reaching high max strength, explosive strength becomes increasingly important

**Endurance paradox:** Sprinters must still have sufficient endurance to survive mountain stages in the grupetto. Grand Tours are arguably "hardest for the sprinters." Even sprinters need metabolic efficiency to arrive at the final kilometers fresh.

### 12.6 Amateur/Competitive Cyclist Scaling

**Scaling principle:** Amateurs must make every session count. Can't afford junk miles with 8-12 hours/week.

**Recommended approach by experience:**
- Build aerobic base with sweet spot (pyramidal distribution)
- Then switch to polarized for race-specific phase
- 2-3 intensity days/week, spaced with Z2 and recovery rides

**Training hours by goal:**

| Goal | Hours/Week | Weeks Preparation |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Recreational fitness / short events | 6-8 | Ongoing |
| Gran Fondo completion | 6-10 | 12-16 |
| Competitive Gran Fondo / Cat 3-4 | 8-12 | 12-16 |
| High-level amateur / ultra-distance | 10-15 | 16-24 |
| Elite amateur | 12+ | Year-round |

**Gran Fondo prep template (8-12 hrs/week, 12-16 weeks):**
- 3-week loading blocks + 1-week recovery
- Long rides build to 60-70% of target distance
- Key intervals: VO2max (4-8 min) + Z2 endurance + sweet spot
- Expected: FTP +5-20%, CTL +40-60%

**Cat 3/4 racing template (7-10 hrs/week):**
- 4-week blocks: 3 loading + 1 recovery
- Crit-specific: high-cadence bursts, sprint timing, repeated efforts
- Sweet spot + sprint intervals (6-12 reps of 10-30s max, 2-4 min recovery)

### 12.7 Peaking & Tapering

**Three-week taper structure:**

| Week | Name | Volume | Intensity |
|------|------|--------|-----------|
| 3 weeks out | Peak Week | ~75% of average | Highest -- final hard block |
| 2 weeks out | Controlled Drop | Reduced 20-30% | Maintained |
| Race week | Short & Sharp | Reduced 40-60% | Neuromuscular prep only |

**Openers workout (day before race):**
- Duration: 1-1.5 hours
- Structure: Z2 + 4x 90s at FTP + 2x 6s all-out sprint
- Alternative: 3-4x 30s sprints within easy ride

**Race week example (Sunday race):**
- Mon: Rest or very easy spin
- Tue: Abridged interval session (fewer sets than normal)
- Wed: Easy Z2, 1-1.5 hrs
- Thu: Rest
- Fri: Openers
- Sat: Rest or 30-min easy spin
- Sun: RACE

**Expected performance gain from proper taper:** 3-5%

## 13. Training Load Monitoring & Adaptation

### 13.1 Key Metrics

| Metric | What It Tells You | How to Track |
|--------|------------------|--------------|
| **RPE** | Most sensitive single indicator of internal load | Session RPE (1-10) after every workout |
| **HRV** | Autonomic nervous system recovery state | Morning measurement; 7-day rolling average |
| **Resting HR** | Recovery status; less variable than HRV day-to-day | Morning measurement; week-averaged |
| **Wellness scores** | Early warning before performance drops | 5 daily questions: fatigue, stress, DOMS, sleep, mood |
| **Power/HR decoupling** | Aerobic drift, fatigue, fitness level | Monitor during long rides |
| **TSS/CTL/ATL/TSB** | Training load, fitness, fatigue, form | TrainingPeaks or similar platform |

### 13.2 Performance Management Chart (PMC)

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **TSS** | f(NP, IF, duration) | 1 hr at FTP = 100 TSS |
| **CTL (Fitness)** | 42-day rolling avg of daily TSS | Build 3-7 TSS/week |
| **ATL (Fatigue)** | 7-day rolling avg of daily TSS | Monitor for excessive spikes |
| **TSB (Form)** | CTL - ATL | Race day: +5 to +25 |

**Limitations:** Not all TSS is equal (threshold ride vs. endurance ride); requires accurate FTP; doesn't account for life stress, sleep, illness. Use PMC as a guide alongside subjective measures, never as the sole decision-maker.

### 13.3 Overtraining Continuum

| Stage | Duration to Recover | Signs |
|-------|---------------------|-------|
| **Functional overreaching** | Days | Deliberate; accept short-term fatigue for supercompensation |
| **Non-functional overreaching** | Weeks | Stacked stress without recovery; performance decline |
| **Overtraining syndrome** | Weeks to months | Chronic maladaptation; hormonal disruption |

**Four major warning signs:**
1. Resting HR elevated >3-5 BPM for multiple days
2. HRV depressed below normal range for >1 week
3. RPE consistently elevated relative to power output
4. Inability to reach target power outputs despite adequate training

**When to reduce load:**
- RPE/power mismatch persisting >3 sessions
- HRV depressed for >1 week
- RHR elevated >3-5 BPM from baseline for multiple days
- Wellness scores trending negative across multiple dimensions
- "I do not feel like training" becoming daily

**Return-to-hard-training criteria:**
10 consecutive mornings with normal RHR + normal 7-day HRV average + 2 successful submaximal efforts at 85-90% of pre-fatigue normalized power.

### 13.4 Blood Biomarker Monitoring

Test every 4-6 weeks under consistent conditions (fasted, morning, before training, day after rest).

| Biomarker | Optimal Range (Athletes) | Significance |
|-----------|-------------------------|--------------|
| Ferritin | >= 100 ng/mL | Underpins erythropoiesis; critical for altitude adaptation |
| Testosterone (male) | 500-800 ng/dL | Protein synthesis, RBC production, glycogen replenishment |
| Cortisol | Context-dependent | Chronically elevated = catabolic, immunosuppressive |
| T:C ratio | Higher = better | Greater anabolic drive; positive training outcomes |
| Vitamin D | 50-80 ng/mL | Bone health, immune function, muscle function |
| CRP | Low | Inflammation marker |
| Creatine Kinase | Context-dependent | Muscle damage marker |
| TSH | 1.0-2.5 mIU/L | Thyroid function |

**Key finding:** Male athletes with testosterone in the lower quartile had 4.5x higher stress fracture rate, even within normal clinical range.

## 14. Altitude & Heat Protocols

### 14.1 Altitude Training

**Optimal altitude:** 2,000-2,500m (above 2,000m, below 3,000m)

**Key locations used by WorldTour teams:**

| Location | Altitude | Used By |
|----------|----------|---------|
| Tenerife / Teide | ~2,300m | Visma (pre-Giro), Ineos, most teams |
| Sierra Nevada, Spain | 2,320m | Visma (pre-Tour), UAE, multiple teams |
| Livigno, Italy | 1,816m | Various teams |
| St. Moritz, Switzerland | 1,822m | Various teams |
| Park City, Utah | 2,134m | Visma (pre-Vuelta) |

**Protocol:**
- Minimum 14 days; optimal 3-4 weeks
- Live high, train low when possible (preserve training quality)
- First few days gentle; intense training avoided initially
- Ensure adequate iron stores (ferritin >= 100 ng/mL)
- Individualize -- acclimatization varies from 4 days to 1+ week

**Expected adaptations:**

| Parameter | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| EPO peak | Within 1-2 days of arrival |
| Hemoglobin mass increase | ~2.9% after 11 days; ~3.5% after 19 days |
| Total hemoglobin (3-4 weeks) | ~8% increase |
| VO2max | ~4% increase |
| Benefits post-descent | Hb_mass elevated up to 3 weeks; performance window 1-4 weeks |

### 14.2 Heat Acclimation

**Why:** Core temperature rise = ~1% drop in gross efficiency per degree C (~10-12W loss).

**Methods:**
1. **Active heat training:** Turbo trainer without fan, heated room, extra layers; target core temp >= 38.5 C for 45-75 min
2. **Passive (hot water immersion / sauna):** 40 C bath for 30 min or 90 C dry sauna for 10-15 min post-exercise

**Protocol:**
- Adaptations begin in 5-9 days; near full acclimation at 2 weeks
- Do not go more than 2-3 days between sessions (decay rate ~2.5%/day)
- Two-phase approach: first block ~6 weeks before race, maintenance every 2-3 days, final block in last 6-10 days

**Adaptations gained:**
- Earlier sweating onset, improved thermoregulation
- Increased plasma volume
- Better heart rate regulation during heat
- Decreased sodium in sweat

## 15. Power Analysis & Performance Metrics

### 15.1 Power Duration Curve (PDC)

Plots peak power across all durations. Used for:
- **Rider phenotyping:** Sprinter, pursuiter, time trialist, all-rounder based on PDC shape
- **Limiter identification:** Where does the curve drop off relative to targets?
- **Progress tracking:** Compare PDC curves across training blocks

### 15.2 Key Power Metrics

| Metric | What It Represents | How to Use |
|--------|-------------------|------------|
| **FTP** | ~1-hour sustainable power | Primary training anchor; test monthly |
| **Pmax** | Peak sprint power | Track neuromuscular development |
| **FRC** (Functional Reserve Capacity) | Work above FTP before exhaustion (~W') | Determines duration of above-threshold efforts |
| **mFTP** (Modeled FTP) | FTP derived from PDC modeling | Cross-reference with tested FTP |
| **NP** (Normalized Power) | "Effective" power accounting for variability | More meaningful than average power for variable rides |
| **IF** (Intensity Factor) | NP/FTP ratio | Quantifies relative intensity of a session |
| **VI** (Variability Index) | NP/AP ratio | Pacing consistency; closer to 1.0 = more even |

### 15.3 Fatigue Resistance / Durability

Compare PDC curves at different energy expenditure levels (e.g., fresh vs. after 3,000 kJ vs. after 5,000 kJ). Shows how power declines with accumulated fatigue -- critical for Grand Tour performance.

**How to train it:**
- Place intervals at the end of long rides (threshold work in hours 3-5)
- Back-to-back training days with intensity on day 2
- Progressive overload of total ride duration before key intervals

### 15.4 Testing Protocol

**Recommended FTP test:** 20-minute all-out effort (multiply average power by 0.95), or ramp test as secondary option. Test in consistent conditions (rested, fueled, same time of day, same equipment).

**Testing frequency:** Every 4-8 weeks during build phases; at minimum every 12 weeks.

**INSCYD / metabolic test frequency:** 4-6 times per year for serious athletes; at minimum at start and mid-point of each macrocycle.
