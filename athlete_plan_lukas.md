## Off-Bike Protocols

### A. Daily Hip Mobility Routine (5 minutes -- non-negotiable)

Do this every day, ideally after sitting for extended periods at work. These 5 exercises directly counter the damage of desk sitting.

**1. 90/90 Hip Switches -- 5 reps each side**
- Sit on floor, front shin perpendicular to your body (knee at 90 degrees), rear shin parallel behind you (also 90 degrees). Lean your torso forward over the front knee.
- **Feel it in:** Deep stretch in the OUTER HIP (piriformis/glute) of the front leg. Stretch in the INNER THIGH (adductor) of the back leg. If you lean forward, you should feel a deep pull in the front hip's external rotators.
- Switch sides by rotating both legs simultaneously (windshield wiper motion).
- Hold each side 15 seconds, then switch.

**2. Couch Stretch (Hip Flexor) -- 45 seconds each side**
- Kneel with one foot against a wall or couch behind you (top of foot against surface, knee on floor close to wall). Step the other foot forward into a lunge.
- Squeeze the GLUTE of the back leg and push your hips forward. Stay upright -- don't lean forward.
- **Feel it in:** Deep stretch in the FRONT OF THE HIP and upper QUAD of the back leg. You should feel the stretch specifically where the hip flexor (iliopsoas) crosses the front of the hip joint. If you squeeze the glute hard, the stretch intensifies.
- This is the #1 exercise for desk workers. Your hip flexors are shortened 8+ hours/day.

**3. Cat-Cow -- 8 reps**
- Hands and knees position. Inhale: drop belly, lift head, arch back (cow). Exhale: round back, tuck chin, push floor away (cat).
- **Feel it in:** Cow: gentle stretch across the ABDOMINALS and CHEST. Cat: stretch across the UPPER BACK (between shoulder blades) and LOWER BACK. The movement should feel like a wave traveling up the spine.
- Go slow. Spend 3 seconds in each position.

**4. Hip CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations) -- 3 each direction, each leg**
- Stand on one leg (hold wall for balance). Lift the other knee to hip height. Slowly draw the largest possible circle with your knee: up, out to side, back behind you, and return. Reverse direction.
- **Feel it in:** You'll feel different parts of the hip "grab" at different points in the circle. The FRONT of the hip tightens as the leg goes behind you, the OUTER hip (glute med) works as the leg moves sideways, the INNER THIGH stretches as the knee opens outward. The goal is to explore and expand the full range.
- Keep your pelvis STILL. Only the leg moves. If your torso twists, you're compensating.

**5. World's Greatest Stretch -- 3 each side**
- Step into a deep lunge. Place the hand on the same side as the front leg on the floor inside the foot. Rotate your other arm up toward the ceiling, opening the chest.
- **Feel it in:** HIP FLEXOR of the back leg, INNER THIGH (adductor) of the front leg, THORACIC SPINE rotation (mid-back) as you open up, and a stretch across the CHEST/SHOULDER of the rotating arm.
- Hold the rotation for 5 seconds at the top. This single exercise hits nearly every tight area for cyclists.

### B. Tibialis Anterior Rehabilitation Protocol

Your right tibialis anterior weakness is a real limiter on long rides. This progressive program rebuilds strength and endurance in the muscle. Do these 3-4x per week (can combine with Monday strength or daily mobility).

**Phase 1: Activation & Endurance (Weeks 1-4)**

**1. Seated Toe Raises -- 3 x 20 reps**
- Sit on a chair, feet flat on floor. Lift ONLY the toes and forefoot off the ground as high as possible, keeping the heel planted. Hold the top position for 1 second.
- **Feel it in:** The muscle running along the OUTER FRONT of your shin (tibialis anterior), from just below the knee to the ankle. You should feel it contracting and working, especially on the right side. If you feel it more in the right than the left, that confirms the weakness.
- Do both legs simultaneously but pay attention to whether the right side fatigues faster.

**2. Heel Walks -- 3 x 20 steps**
- Stand up and lift your forefeet off the ground so you're standing on your HEELS only. Walk forward on your heels for 20 steps.
- **Feel it in:** Strong contraction in the FRONT OF BOTH SHINS. The tibialis anterior must work continuously to keep the forefoot elevated. Your right side will likely fatigue faster -- that's the point. Stop if you get cramping.

**3. Banded Dorsiflexion -- 3 x 15 reps each foot**
- Sit with legs extended. Loop a resistance band around the top of your right forefoot. Anchor the band behind you (around a table leg or have someone hold it). Pull your toes toward your shin against the band's resistance, then slowly release.
- **Feel it in:** The tibialis anterior working through its full range. The CONTRACTION should be on the front outer shin as you pull the toes up. The ECCENTRIC (lowering) phase is where the real rehab happens -- take 3 seconds to lower.

**Phase 2: Strength & Load (Weeks 5-8, or when Phase 1 feels easy)**

**4. Wall Tibialis Raises -- 3 x 15-20 reps**
- Lean your back flat against a wall. Walk your feet out about 30-40cm from the wall. Keeping your back against the wall, raise your toes as high as possible, then lower slowly.
- **Feel it in:** Strong burn in the FRONT OF THE SHINS. This is harder than seated raises because your body weight creates more resistance. The further your feet are from the wall, the harder it is.
- Progress by moving feet further from wall.

**5. Single-Leg Eccentric Dorsiflexion -- 3 x 10 reps (right leg only)**
- Sit on edge of a chair. Raise the right forefoot up (concentric), then take 5 seconds to lower it back down (eccentric). The slow lowering is the therapeutic part.
- **Feel it in:** Controlled tension along the ENTIRE front of the shin. It should feel like a deep, fatiguing burn -- not sharp pain. If you feel sharp pain, stop.

### C. Monday Strength + Mobility Circuit (30-40 minutes)

This is your primary off-bike session. With 2x 12kg kettlebells + bands, the focus is on single-leg work (makes the weight more challenging) and cycling-specific movements. If/when you get a 24kg KB, progression notes are included.

**Warm-Up (3 min):**
- Hip CARs: 3 each direction, each leg
- Banded monster walks: 10 steps each direction
- Glute bridges: 10 reps with 2-second hold at top

**Circuit (repeat 2-3 rounds, 25-30 min):**

**1. Goblet Squat -- 3 x 10-12 reps**
- Hold one or both KBs at chest height (12kg or 24kg). Feet shoulder width, toes slightly out. Squat deep -- elbows inside knees at bottom.
- **Feel it in:** QUADS (front of thigh) drive you up. GLUTES (butt) engage heavily at the bottom and as you stand. You should feel the weight of the kettlebell pulling your chest forward, which forces your CORE to brace hard to stay upright.
- *With 24kg KB:* Hold with both hands in goblet position. This becomes a legitimate strength exercise.
- Tempo: 3 seconds down, pause 1 second at bottom, explode up.

**2. Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift (KB) -- 3 x 8 each leg**
- Hold a 12kg KB in the hand OPPOSITE to the standing leg. Hinge at the hip, sending the non-standing leg straight behind you as your torso tilts forward. KB hangs straight down. Return to standing.
- **Feel it in:** HAMSTRING and GLUTE of the standing leg should be stretched and loaded as you hinge forward. The LOWER BACK and CORE work to prevent rotation (your torso wants to twist toward the KB side -- resist this). The GLUTE MED (outer hip) of the standing leg fires to keep your pelvis level.
- This exercise also challenges ANKLE STABILITY -- good for your tibialis anterior rehab.

**3. KB Swing -- 3 x 15 reps**
- Both hands on one KB (12kg, or 24kg when available). Feet wider than shoulders. Hinge at hips, swing KB between legs, then drive hips forward explosively to swing KB to chest height. Arms stay relaxed -- power comes from the hips.
- **Feel it in:** The SNAP should come from your GLUTES and HAMSTRINGS as your hips drive forward. NOT from your arms or lower back. At the top of the swing, squeeze your glutes hard and brace your abs (imagine someone is about to punch your stomach). You should NOT feel this in your lower back -- if you do, you're hinging too late or lifting with the back.

**4. Bulgarian Split Squat -- 3 x 8 each leg**
- Rear foot elevated on a chair or couch. Front foot about 60-70cm ahead. Hold KBs at sides (12kg each) or one KB in goblet position.
- **Feel it in:** The QUAD and GLUTE of the front leg do all the work. Deep stretch in the HIP FLEXOR of the back leg at the bottom position. You should feel like the front leg is doing 80%+ of the effort. If your front knee is tracking over your toes, your stance is too narrow -- step the front foot further out.
- This is your most important strength exercise. It builds single-leg power directly relevant to pedaling and corrects bilateral imbalances.

**5. Push-Up -- 3 x 12-15 reps**
- Full plank position. Hands slightly wider than shoulders. Lower chest to fist-height off floor. Press back up.
- **Feel it in:** CHEST (pectorals) and TRICEPS do the pressing. But if done correctly, you should also feel your CORE braced the entire time -- abs tight, glutes squeezed, body rigid as a plank. If your hips sag, your core isn't engaged.

**6. Banded Pull-Apart -- 3 x 15 reps**
- Hold resistance band at arm's length in front of you, hands shoulder width. Pull the band apart by squeezing your shoulder blades together until the band touches your chest.
- **Feel it in:** The muscles BETWEEN your shoulder blades (rhomboids, mid-trapezius, rear deltoids). This directly counteracts the rounded-forward desk and cycling posture. Squeeze and hold for 1 second at full contraction.

**7. Pallof Press (Band) -- 2 x 10 each side**
- Anchor band at chest height to a door handle or sturdy object. Stand sideways to anchor point, hold band at chest with both hands. Press hands straight forward, extending arms fully, then return to chest. The band tries to rotate you -- resist it.
- **Feel it in:** Your OBLIQUES and deep CORE (transversus abdominis) fighting the rotational pull. You should NOT rotate at all. Keep hips square, shoulders square. This trains the anti-rotation stability you need for efficient power transfer on the bike.

**8. Dead Bug -- 2 x 8 each side**
- Lie on back, arms pointing to ceiling, knees bent at 90 degrees (shins parallel to floor). Slowly extend one arm overhead while extending the opposite leg out straight, hovering above the floor. Return and switch sides.
- **Feel it in:** Your DEEP ABDOMINALS (transversus abdominis) working to keep your lower back PRESSED FLAT against the floor. The moment your lower back arches off the floor, you've lost the exercise. Stop the range of motion before that happens. Think about pulling your belly button to the floor.

**Cool-Down (5 min):**
- Couch stretch: 45s each side
- Pigeon pose: 45s each side
- Foam rolling quads + hip flexors: 60s each

## Race-Specific Strategy Notes

### Eschborn-Frankfurt Pacing
- Start conservatively. The Feldberg comes in the middle of the race -- don't burn matches early.
- Target Feldberg at 85-90% FTP (sweet spot) for the first 8km, then assess if you can push to threshold for the final 3km.
- Mammolshainer Stich: short and steep -- go hard but know it's brief. Recover immediately after.
- Fuel early and often -- first gel/bar by km 20.

### Rund um Koeln Pacing
- Home turf advantage -- you know this terrain from BTS rides.
- The 27% Agathaberg is short but savage. Get in a low gear early, stay seated, spin.
- Schloss Bensberg cobbles: grip the bars firmly, relax your upper body, let the bike move under you.
- This is a repeated-efforts race: surge, recover, surge, recover. Don't let gaps open.

### RAD RACE ONE TWENTY Strategy
- 2,230m of climbing in 126km = average 1.8% gradient. But it's alpine = longer sustained climbs.
- Pace conservatively on the first climbs. The race is won (or lost) in the second half.
- Fueling is CRITICAL: 90-100g carbs/hour. Start eating at km 10.
- If you do the team time trial on Saturday, treat it as an opener -- don't go beyond 90% effort.
